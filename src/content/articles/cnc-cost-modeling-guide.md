  <h2 id="faq-cnc-cost-modeling">FAQ: CNC Cost Modeling</h2>
  
  <h3 id="what-is-cnc-cost-modeling">What is CNC cost modeling?</h3>
  <p><strong>CNC cost modeling</strong> is a systematic approach to estimating and optimizing the costs associated with CNC machining. It calculates material, labor, tooling, machine time, overhead, and quality factors to provide highly accurate per-part costs, helping engineers make informed design and process choices.</p>
  
  <h3 id="how-does-cnc-economic-analysis-differ-from-standard-estimati">How does CNC economic analysis differ from standard estimation?</h3>
  <p>Standard estimation uses static rates. CNC economic analysis is dynamic, incorporating real-time data like tool wear, energy consumption, and machine utilization. It allows for scenario testing, giving you a flexible and accurate cost model.</p>
  
  <h3 id="what-are-the-key-components-of-a-cnc-cost-function">What are the key components of a CNC cost function?</h3>
  <p>A comprehensive CNC cost function includes: setup cost + machining cost (time × hourly rate) + tooling cost + material cost + quality cost (inspection &amp; rework) + overhead. Advanced models also factor in tool life and machine depreciation.</p>
