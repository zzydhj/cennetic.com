  <h2 id="what-is-cad-computer-aided-design-basics">What is CAD? Computer-Aided Design Basics</h2>
  <p>CAD stands for Computer-Aided Design. It is the software used by engineers and designers to create precise 2D drawings or 3D models of parts and products. For CNC beginners, think of CAD as the digital blueprint that dictates the exact geometry of your final product. Without CAD, you would have to manually plot coordinates—a tedious and highly error-prone process. With CAD, you can design complex geometries, simulate assemblies, and make instant modifications.</p>

  <h2 id="cad-vs-cam-getting-cad-for-cnc-explained">CAD vs CAM: Getting CAD for CNC Explained</h2>
  <p>A common hurdle for beginners is understanding the difference between CAD and CAM. While CAD focuses on the <em>design</em> of the part, CAM (Computer-Aided Manufacturing) converts that design into machine-readable instructions (G-code) that tell the CNC machine how to move. Here is a clear breakdown:</p>

  <h3 id="faq-cad-for-cnc-explained-for-beginners">FAQ: CAD for CNC Explained for Beginners</h3>
  <p><strong>What is the best CAD software for CNC beginners?</strong><br>
  For beginners, we highly recommend Fusion 360 or Onshape. Both offer user-friendly interfaces, integrated CAM features, and free versions for hobbyists or startups.</p>

  <p><strong>Do I need to learn CAD before using a CNC machine?</strong><br>
  Yes, a CNC machine requires a digital design to follow. While some machines come with pre-loaded test files, creating your own custom parts requires basic CAD knowledge.</p>

  <p><strong>Can I use any CAD software with Cennetic machines?</strong><br>
  Absolutely. Cennetic machines are compatible with any CAD/CAM software that can output standard G-code. As long as your software can export standard formats (like .STEP or .DXF) to your CAM program, our machines will cut it flawlessly.</p>
