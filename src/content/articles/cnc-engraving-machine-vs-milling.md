<h2 id="1-cnc-engraving-machine-vs-cnc-milling-machine-core-differen">1. CNC Engraving Machine vs CNC Milling Machine: Core Differences in Design and Application</h2>
<p>The key distinction between a <strong>CNC engraving machine</strong> and a CNC milling machine lies in spindle speed, cutting depth, tooling resolution, and intended material. A CNC engraving machine operates at high RPM (up to 24,000) with fine-diameter tools (as small as 0.1mm), optimized for shallow, detailed surface work on acrylic, aluminum, brass, and wood. A CNC milling machine runs at lower RPM with higher torque, designed for deep material removal on steel, iron, and thick alloys.</p>
<p>For advertising and crafts businesses — where the output is signage, nameplates, decorative panels, and custom gifts — the <strong>CNC engraving machine</strong> is almost always the more productive and cost-effective choice. The milling machine becomes necessary only when your work involves structural metal components or deep 3D contouring in hard materials.</p>

<h2 id="6-frequently-asked-questions-about-cnc-engraving-machines">6. Frequently Asked Questions About CNC Engraving Machines</h2>
<h3 id="can-a-cnc-engraving-machine-cut-through-metal">Can a CNC engraving machine cut through metal?</h3>
<p>Yes, but within limits. A <strong>CNC engraving machine</strong> is designed for shallow material removal — typically up to 2mm per pass — on non-ferrous metals like aluminum, brass, and copper. It excels at engraving text, logos, serial numbers, and decorative patterns on metal surfaces. For cutting thick metal plate or machining structural steel components, a CNC milling machine is the appropriate tool. Cennetic’s engraving machines can handle materials up to HRC 45 hardness with correct tooling selection.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-difference-between-cnc-engraving-and-cnc-milling">What is the difference between CNC engraving and CNC milling?</h3>
<p>CNC engraving uses a high-speed rotating tool to remove material in shallow passes, prioritizing surface detail, fine resolution, and aesthetic finish. CNC milling involves deeper cuts to shape a workpiece in three dimensions, prioritizing material removal rate and structural accuracy. For advertising and crafts applications — signs, plaques, awards, decorative panels — a <strong>CNC engraving machine</strong> is the correct choice. For precision metal components used in machinery or automotive parts, a mill is required.</p>
