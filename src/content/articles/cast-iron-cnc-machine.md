  <h2 id="1-why-frame-material-matters-for-heavy-cutting">1. Why Frame Material Matters for Heavy Cutting</h2>

  <p>
    In a heavy cutting processing factory, every micron of precision counts.
    Steel frames, while offering higher Young’s modulus (stiffness), transmit
    vibrations efficiently — meaning energy from interrupted cuts, tool engagement
    variations, and spindle harmonics travels through the structure and directly
    affects the cutting zone. The result: chatter marks, dimensional deviation,
    and accelerated tool wear.
  </p>
  <p>
    Cast iron behaves fundamentally differently. Its microstructure — a matrix
    of iron with dispersed graphite flakes — converts vibrational energy into
    heat through internal friction. This is why a <strong>cast iron CNC
    machine</strong> feels “dead” under impact compared to a steel-frame machine
    that rings. For heavy cutting of steel, stainless, titanium, and cast
    components, this damping characteristic is the single most important
    structural property.
  </p>

  <h2 id="6-faq-cast-iron-cnc-machine">6. FAQ: Cast Iron CNC Machine</h2>

  <h3 id="is-a-cast-iron-cnc-machine-better-than-a-steel-frame-for-hea">Is a cast iron CNC machine better than a steel frame for heavy cutting?</h3>
  <p>
    Yes, for heavy cutting applications, a <strong>cast iron CNC machine</strong>
    is generally the superior choice. Its internal damping ratio is 8–10×
    higher than structural steel, which directly reduces chatter, improves
    surface finish, and extends tool life. Steel frames may be preferred for
    high-speed light-duty operations — such as aluminum aerospace parts —
    where low inertia is more important than damping.
  </p>

  <h3 id="how-does-a-cast-iron-cnc-machine-improve-surface-finish">How does a cast iron CNC machine improve surface finish?</h3>
  <p>
    By absorbing vibrational energy within its graphite-matrix microstructure,
    cast iron prevents chatter marks from forming on the workpiece surface.
    In practical terms, a cast iron CNC machine can achieve Ra 0.4–0.8 µm
    on heavy interrupted cuts where a comparable steel-frame machine would
    produce Ra 1.6–3.2 µm — often eliminating the need for secondary finishing
    operations entirely.
  </p>

  <h3 id="are-cast-iron-cnc-machines-more-expensive-to-maintain">Are cast iron CNC machines more expensive to maintain?</h3>
  <p>
    No. Cast iron machines typically require <em>less</em> maintenance than
    steel-frame equivalents in heavy cutting environments. The natural graphite
    in cast iron provides self-lubrication on slideway surfaces, reducing wear.
    The material’s resistance to fatigue and deformation also means geometric
    accuracy is maintained longer between calibration intervals. Foundation
    and installation costs may be higher due to the greater mass, but
    operational maintenance costs are lower.
  </p>

  <h3 id="what-is-the-typical-lifespan-of-a-cast-iron-cnc-machine">What is the typical lifespan of a cast iron CNC machine?</h3>
  <p>
    With a proper preventive maintenance program, a cast iron CNC machine
    can deliver accurate performance for <strong>20–30 years</strong>. The
    key factors are regular slideway lubrication, periodic spindle bearing
    inspection, and annual geometric calibration. QiaoFeng’s stress-relief
    annealed frames are specifically designed to maintain dimensional stability
    throughout this service life.
  </p>

  <h3 id="can-a-cast-iron-cnc-machine-handle-high-speed-machining">Can a cast iron CNC machine handle high-speed machining?</h3>
  <p>
    A cast iron CNC machine excels in heavy cutting at moderate-to-high
    spindle speeds. For very high-speed machining of aluminum (spindle speeds
    above 15,000 RPM), polymer concrete or steel frames with active damping
    may be preferred due to lower rotational inertia. However, for hard
    materials — steel, stainless, titanium, Inconel — cast iron’s damping
    advantage is decisive at any practical cutting speed.
  </p>
